Guide 1 · Domains

Buy a domain from Porkbun or Namecheap and connect it to Dredge

Your domain lives at a registrar, your site lives on Dredge. Connecting them means telling the registrar to send visitors to us, which you do by changing the domain's nameservers. It takes about five minutes of clicking and up to 48 hours for the change to spread across the internet.

Before you start

  • A Dredge plan. If you don't have one yet, pick a plan and, when checkout asks about a domain, choose the option to use a domain you already own and type in the domain you're about to buy (or already have).
  • Your Dredge nameservers. They're in the welcome email we send when your account is set up, and in your client area at my.dredgehost.com under your hosting service. There are two of them and they look like ns1.example.com.

Step 1: Buy the domain

  1. Go to porkbun.com or namecheap.com and search for the name you want.
  2. Add it to your cart. Keep WHOIS privacy switched on (both include it free). You can skip any hosting, email, or website-builder add-ons; Dredge covers those.
  3. Check out and confirm the verification email the registrar sends you. ICANN requires this, and an unverified domain can be suspended after 15 days.

Step 2a: Change the nameservers at Porkbun

  1. Log in and open Account → Domain Management.
  2. Find your domain and hover over or click it to show its Details.
  3. Next to Authoritative Nameservers, click Edit.
  4. Delete the Porkbun nameservers that are there and paste in your two Dredge nameservers, one per line.
  5. Click Submit.

Step 2b: Change the nameservers at Namecheap

  1. Log in and open Domain List from the left-hand menu.
  2. Click Manage next to your domain.
  3. In the Nameservers section, change the dropdown from “Namecheap BasicDNS” to Custom DNS.
  4. Enter your two Dredge nameservers, one in each box.
  5. Click the green checkmark to save.

Step 3: Wait, then check

Nameserver changes usually show up within an hour or two, but can take up to 48 hours. Once they do:

  • Visiting your domain shows your Dredge site (or the default cPanel page if you haven't uploaded anything yet).
  • Free TLS kicks in by itself. cPanel's AutoSSL checks for newly pointed domains a few times a day and issues a certificate, so https:// starts working without you doing anything. If it hasn't after a day, open SSL/TLS Status in cPanel and click Run AutoSSL.

Adding a second domain to the same account

On Channel and Harbor you can host more than one site. Point the extra domain's nameservers at Dredge exactly as above, then in cPanel open Domains → Create A New Domain, enter the domain, and untick “Share document root” so it gets its own folder. Anything you put in that folder is what the new domain shows.

Want to keep DNS at your registrar instead? You can, but then you're responsible for keeping the records in sync with us, including the ones email needs. Changing nameservers is simpler and is what we support by default. If you have a reason to do it the other way, get in touch and we'll send you the exact records.

Guide 2 · WordPress

Move a WordPress site to Dredge

Moving WordPress means copying two things: the files (your theme, plugins, and uploaded images) and the database (your posts, pages, and settings). There are two ways to do it. Try the plugin method first; use the manual method if your site is large or the plugin struggles.

Before you start

  • Don't cancel your old host yet. Keep it running until the site is live on Dredge and you've checked it for a few days.
  • Take a full backup on your old host, even if you think you won't need it.
  • Check the size of the site against your plan's storage on the pricing page.

Method 1: With a migration plugin

  1. Install a fresh WordPress on Dredge. In cPanel, open WordPress Manager (or Softaculous Apps Installer), choose WordPress, pick your domain, and install. Leave the directory field empty so it installs at the top of the domain.
  2. Export from the old site. On the old site's dashboard, go to Plugins → Add New, install All-in-One WP Migration, then open it and choose Export → File. Download the .wpress file it creates.
  3. Import on Dredge. Log in to the new WordPress on Dredge, install the same plugin, choose Import, and upload the file. Confirm that it will overwrite the fresh install.
  4. Log in again with your old site's username and password, since the import brought your users over. Then go to Settings → Permalinks and click Save twice to rebuild your links.

The free version of the plugin limits how big an import can be. If your export is over the limit, use Method 2 below rather than paying for an extension.

Method 2: By hand

  1. Download the files. On your old host, use its file manager or an FTP app such as FileZilla to download the whole WordPress folder, usually public_html. If the file manager can compress it to a .zip first, do that; it's much faster.
  2. Export the database. On the old host, open phpMyAdmin, select your WordPress database, click Export, leave it on “Quick” and “SQL”, and download the file. Not sure which database? Open wp-config.php and look for DB_NAME.
  3. Create a database on Dredge. In cPanel, open Database Wizard. Create a database, create a user with a strong password, and give that user All Privileges. Write down all three names and the password.
  4. Import the database. In cPanel, open phpMyAdmin, click your new database on the left, choose Import, and upload the .sql file.
  5. Upload the files. In cPanel, open File Manager, go to public_html (or your extra domain's folder), upload the .zip, then right-click it and choose Extract. Make sure wp-config.php ends up directly inside the folder, not in a subfolder.
  6. Point WordPress at the new database. Edit wp-config.php and update these three lines with what you wrote down in step 3: 
    define( 'DB_NAME', 'youraccount_wpdb' );
define( 'DB_USER', 'youraccount_wpuser' );
define( 'DB_PASSWORD', 'the-password-you-chose' );
    Leave DB_HOST as localhost.

Preview it before you switch

Your domain still points at the old host at this point, which is good: visitors see nothing change while you check the copy. To look at the Dredge copy yourself, add a line to your computer's hosts file with your Dredge server's IP address (shown in cPanel under Server Information) followed by your domain, for example:

203.0.113.10  yourdomain.com www.yourdomain.com

On Windows the file is C:\Windows\System32\drivers\etc\hosts; on Mac and Linux it's /etc/hosts. Browse the site, log in, and click around. Remove the line when you're done.

Go live

  1. Change your domain's nameservers to Dredge, as in Guide 1.
  2. Once the change has spread and AutoSSL has issued your certificate, check that the site loads over https:// and that images, forms, and logins work.
  3. If the domain name itself changed during the move, install the Better Search Replace plugin and replace the old address with the new one across all tables.
  4. Give it a few days, take one last backup from the old host, and then cancel it.

Stuck partway through? Email support@dredgehost.com with your domain and where you got to. The people who run the servers will pick it up.

Guide 3 · Email

Set up email on your domain

Every Dredge plan includes mailboxes on your own domain, like you@yourdomain.com. This works once your domain's nameservers point at Dredge (see Guide 1), because that's what tells the rest of the internet to deliver your mail to us.

Step 1: Create a mailbox

  1. In cPanel, open Email Accounts and click Create.
  2. Pick the domain, type the part before the @, and set a strong password.
  3. Set the storage limit. Mail counts toward your plan's disk space, so give each mailbox a sensible cap rather than “Unlimited”.
  4. Click Create.

Step 2: Read it in your browser

Go to https://yourdomain.com/webmail and log in with the full email address and its password. That's all you need to send and receive from anywhere.

Step 3: Add it to your phone or computer

In cPanel, next to the mailbox in Email Accounts, click Connect Devices. It shows the exact server names for your account, and can generate automatic setup files for Apple Mail, iPhone, and Outlook. If you'd rather enter it by hand, most apps want:

SettingValue
UsernameYour full email address
Incoming (IMAP)Server from Connect Devices, port 993, SSL/TLS
Outgoing (SMTP)Same server, port 465, SSL/TLS, authentication on
PasswordThe mailbox password

Choose IMAP over POP3 so your mail stays in sync between your phone, laptop, and webmail.

Step 4: Make sure your mail isn't marked as spam

In cPanel, open Email Deliverability. If it shows problems next to your domain, click Repair. That sets up SPF and DKIM, the records that prove your mail really came from you. Then send a test message to a Gmail or Outlook address and check it arrives in the inbox.

Handy extras

  • Forwarders send mail for one address to another, such as hello@ to your personal inbox, without a separate mailbox.
  • Autoresponders send an automatic reply while you're away.
  • Spam Filters can move likely spam to a folder instead of your inbox.

Outgoing email has an hourly cap on every plan to protect the server's reputation. It's plenty for everyday mail and site notifications, but not for newsletters. For bulk mail, use a dedicated service and keep your Dredge mailbox for one-to-one mail.
